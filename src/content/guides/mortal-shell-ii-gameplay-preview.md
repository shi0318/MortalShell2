If you are searching for a **Mortal Shell II gameplay preview**, the Open Beta gives a useful but deliberately limited answer. The official Beta listing covers the Prologue and the first explorable region, roughly the opening three hours. It is enough to test the combat rhythm and the idea of Shell selection; it is not a complete review of the retail world, final balance, or endgame.

## What the Open Beta actually covers

The official Steam Open Beta listing describes the **Prologue** and the first explorable region. Its approximate three-hour description is a scope marker, not a promise that every player will finish it in exactly three hours. The Beta is an in-development build, so its enemy placement, numbers, performance, and route details should be labelled Beta when quoted.

The full game released on **August 20, 2026** on Steam, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X|S. The retail build is therefore a separate evidence point. A Beta route can teach controls, but it cannot establish a final boss order or a guaranteed early build.

## The combat signals that are officially confirmed

Mortal Shell II is presented around fast-paced combat in an interconnected open world. The official material describes Shell possession, posture pressure, critical strikes, powerful sidearms, extensive upgrades, and combat that is not restricted by stamina. Those are the useful pillars for a first test session.

They do not give us final damage values, recovery frames, Shell health, weapon scaling, or a universal best build. Do not copy Harden, Resolve, Riposte, or weapon numbers from the 2020 Mortal Shell into a sequel guide unless the retail build independently confirms them.

## How to test a Shell without inventing a tier list

The official gameplay reveal names **Tiel the Acolyte**, **Eredrim the Venerable**, and **Proxima the Broodseeker**, while the wider game is advertised with eight playable Shells. The public material does not provide a complete final stat sheet for every Shell, so a role-first test is more reliable than ranking them early.

For each Shell you can access, record the same observations:

- how reliably you can recover after a missed attack;
- which action most consistently pressures enemy posture;
- whether a sidearm creates a clear critical window;
- how readable movement and recovery are under pressure;
- which weapon or upgrade choices are actually available at that point.

The result is a personal fit test, not a claim that one Shell is strongest. It also leaves room for balance changes between the Open Beta and the August 20 retail build.

## What the Beta does and does not carry into launch

The official Beta information says the full Beta inventory resets. That includes temporary currency, weapons, Shells, and collectibles gathered in the test. The time spent learning the opening region still matters, but it should not be described as permanent retail progression.

Two participation rewards are explicitly listed: playing the Beta unlocks the **Flayed Harbinger** cosmetic, and progressing beyond **Marrow Keep** unlocks an optional prologue skip for the full game. The official listing does not promise a save transfer for the rest of the inventory or map progress.

## Launch-day checks for this guide

On August 20, record the retail build before converting Beta observations into a formal guide. Check whether the opening Shell availability, the prologue route, enemy placement, sidearm costs, upgrade conditions, and Marrow Keep skip prompt match the Beta. If they differ, keep the Beta note as historical evidence and label the new observation as retail.

## Source boundary

The Open Beta is real playable evidence, but it is still Beta evidence. The official sources support the release date, platform list, Shell count, combat pillars, Beta scope, reset rules, Flayed Harbinger reward, and Marrow Keep condition. They do not support a complete boss route, final Shell tier list, or exact damage table before the retail build is checked.
