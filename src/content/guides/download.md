## Compare the recorded beta with a retail installation

The beta description established a limited starting area, not the complete geography, enemy roster or upgrade economy of the released game. Advice about inventory resets should be read with its original test context. A reset described for a test period does not establish a universal retail save-deletion rule. Equally, a launch reward does not mean the entire beta character state transfers.

Before opening an old executable, identify its Steam app in the library. The retail game is app 2584270; the Open Beta listing used app 4711740. If a guide describes an option you cannot find, first check whether it discusses that separate test build. Do not repair a retail installation by copying undocumented files from the old beta directory.

## When an official link does not offer installation

A historical Steam link may lead to an age gate, login, unavailable product notice or a listing without an install button. Those outcomes require checking the store and account; they do not justify downloading a mirror claiming to preserve the same build. This site supplies no substitute installer and cannot grant access that the platform no longer offers.

If the retail game is missing from your account, inspect purchase history and the account used for the transaction. A remembered beta download is not evidence that you purchased the full game. For a retailer key, resolve activation and regional restrictions with that retailer rather than sending credentials to a third party offering a replacement.

## Preserve data while changing installations

Before uninstalling a test build or resolving a cloud conflict, identify the files and library entries actually involved. Do not delete a folder solely because its name resembles the beta. An undocumented file operation can affect the copy you want to keep. The platform's normal verification tools are preferable to replacing files from an unrelated package.

If you encounter a crash or missing menu after installation, record the build and symptom. Use the [PC diagnosis guide](/pc-requirements/) for performance and display checks. A failure to launch, a missing licence and an inaccessible old test are different problems; reinstalling every version is not a reliable way to distinguish them.

## Choose the correct entry before your first retail session

Open the product entry you intend to play and check its name before selecting Install or Play. Mortal Shell II and Mortal Shell II — Open Beta are separate products. An old Beta shortcut can launch the wrong installation even when the full game is also present. Confirm the Library entry associated with the purchased game, then use that entry for the first retail launch and subsequent updates.

If you purchased an edition with cosmetic content, inspect its listed contents on the receipt and product page. An edition name does not establish a separate campaign executable, and an elapsed advance-access period does not provide a current installation advantage. The [buying and editions guide](/release-date/#read-the-edition-contents-rather-than-the-launch-slogan) explains the difference between permanent listed content and a launch-period benefit.

Before beginning a long session, finish the platform's download and update process. A visible download percentage or pending update should be resolved in the client before you investigate gameplay behaviour. If the title starts successfully but a specific menu fails, keep that symptom separate from ownership and installation. The [Pulse selection guide](/pulse-upgrade-unselectable-beacon/) is an example: the November 16 hotfix addressed mouse selection in that menu, so selecting the correct updated retail build matters more than reinstalling the historical Beta.
