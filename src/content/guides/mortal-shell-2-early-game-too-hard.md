Mortal Shell II's early hours are the most common complaint in its Steam discussions this week. A thread titled "A good game ruined by an abysmal early game filled with one shot trash" was still collecting replies on November 1, "I feel like the game needs some balancing" appeared the same day, and the October threads "Does the game get better later on" and "What's with all the rage bait early on?" describe the same feeling: enemies that hit like a truck, bear traps, exploding enemies and dungeons that end in a miniboss when your heals are already gone. Many replies in those threads say the game eases off once you get over the hump. That is true for a lot of players, but "keep going" is not much help when you are stuck. This guide turns the advice in those threads, and the official changes Cold Symmetry has made since launch, into five checks you can run before deciding the game is not for you.

## Check 1: Are you in the right region?

The single most repeated piece of advice in the current threads is about direction. Several players say they first went south into the Ruins of Mammon and found it brutal, then realised it was not where they were meant to start. One reply in "What's with all the rage bait early on?" puts it plainly: if you travelled south into Mammon you are in the latter half of the game, and the black tendrils covering the launch pad were a warning. Another player in the one-shot thread describes the same experience with the Outskirts of Mammon, and says the game became doable once they turned back.

Our [first hours route](/mortal-shell-2-first-hours/) gives the same direction: from Marrow Keep, take the north portal, because the corrupted south portal is too hard this early. If the enemies around you are taking most of your health in one or two hits and you have only just reached Marrow Keep, the problem may be the route rather than your skill. Go north into Fainweald, claim Tiel at Widow's Overlook, and come back to Mammon later.

A quick way to tell how far most players get is the Steam achievement list. "Home at Last", for reaching Marrowkeep, had been unlocked by 91.4% of players when this guide was written, while the Tiel achievement "Forever Alone?" was at 80.7%. Most people get through the prologue; the drop comes in the first open region, which is exactly where the route matters most.

## Check 2: Do you actually have a Shell?

This sounds like a joke, but it comes up again and again. Harros, the Shell you play in the prologue, is destroyed after the Tar Golem fight and is not one of the eight Shells you keep. After that you are the bare Harbinger until you claim a new body. In the thread "My shell revive meter never builds up", which received another reply on November 3, the answer was exactly this: you lose the first Shell after the tutorial boss and need to find a new one before the meter builds again. In the one-shot thread, another player admits they did not realise you could go to a Shell right away, and played without one for a long time.

If your revive meter never fills, or you seem to die instantly whenever something touches you, check that you are wearing a Shell. The first two most players find are Tiel at Widow's Overlook and Proxima at the Shattered Beacon past Blackridge Pass. The Proxima achievement "Arrival" was at 79.6% when this guide was written. Our [first Shell swap guide](/mortal-shell-2-first-shell-swap/) explains how swapping works at Zhirelle and the one offering mistake that knocks you out of a body.

## Check 3: Pick a Shell that forgives exploration

Once you have a Shell, the next question is whether it suits how you are playing. Players in the current threads recommend three directions. One says early on you need Tiel to maximise damage with her shadow strike, then move to sturdier Shells like Eredrim once you have them. Another suggests Proxima because her Shell is "a little beefier". Two separate replies recommend getting Gragu as soon as possible for exploration, one saying you will never run out of heals, while still switching to another Shell for boss fights.

The Gragu advice ties to a real mechanic. [Gragu, the Insatiable](/shells/gragu/) trades the normal heal for a heart that refills by killing. If your main problem is arriving at a dungeon's miniboss with no healing left, a Shell that heals by fighting changes that maths. Gragu sits in the One-Legged Wolf Tavern and asks for a favour first; the Steam achievement for unlocking him, "Heartless", was at 53.9% when this guide was written. Eredrim's achievement, "Vengeance is Mine", was at 62.0%. Neither is a requirement, but if you keep dying with empty heals, Gragu is the first Shell worth chasing.

## Check 4: Use the tools added since launch

If you bought Mortal Shell II at launch, played a few hours and stopped, several changes may have arrived since you last played. The Week 1 Update on August 29 restricted enemy tracking and magnetism maximums, which speaks directly to the "attacks track you like magnets" complaint in the October thread, and Cold Symmetry said tracking would keep being adjusted per enemy. The same update made riposte damage scale with enemy health, and gave The Monolith a broad pass, including a fix for a bug that caused it to deal double damage on certain hits.

The Week 1 Update also added **Egon's Stone**, which revives you at a dungeon's entrance when you die inside it and spares your Gloom for that death when it breaks. Later hotfixes cleaned up its edge cases, so it is a safety net for the dungeon-ending miniboss problem rather than a gamble. The same update added new Beacons across Fainweald and Mammon that are only active during the day. Merrick's stock also expanded with Egon's Stones, Glimpse Stones and Tarcores once you have gathered enough Ova, and Glimpses and Tarcores are no longer finite.

The biggest option is **Adaptive Difficulty**, added on September 5. It is off by default and can be turned on or off at any time under Game > Game Settings > Adaptive Difficulty. If you are struggling, enemies weaken in damage, health and break resist, and a cap means no single attack can take more than a set fraction of your health bar, so you cannot be killed outright from full health. That is the official answer to "one-shot" complaints. Two cautions: the reductions do not apply during Night Mode, and if you keep winning the system pushes enemies harder. Our [Adaptive Difficulty toggle guide](/mortal-shell-2-adaptive-difficulty-toggle-guide/) covers the setting in detail.

## Check 5: Treat traps and bombers as a puzzle

The "rage bait" thread lists bear traps, a dungeon full of traps, large enemies that explode and smaller exploding enemies that run at you. These are not difficulty spikes in the usual sense; they punish moving quickly through unfamiliar ground. One reply says bear traps can be shot and permanently deactivated, and the official October 13 hotfix notes refer to a bear trap disarm animation, so traps are meant to be dealt with rather than simply avoided. Another reply says only two or three dungeons use tripwires, so the density of gimmicks eases after the opening.

Slow down in a new area, shoot or disarm traps you can see, and pull exploding enemies toward you one at a time instead of fighting near them. If an enemy is about to detonate, backing away is safer than trying to finish it. Ranged sidearms help here; the [sidearms guide](/mortal-shell-2-sidearms-and-ranged-combat/) explains how they fit into a build.

## If it still feels unfair

After the five checks, the remaining difficulty is usually the normal learning curve. Several replies in the current threads describe the same arc: weak at the start, then a threshold where levels, weapon upgrades and Tarstones come together and the game becomes much easier. One player recommends farming a little Gloom and building a couple of levels, then upgrading your weapon, before looking for Tarstone abilities that suit your weapon and sidearm. Our [Gloom farming guide](/mortal-shell-2-gloom-farming-guide/) and [beginner combat tips](/mortal-shell-2-beginner-combat-tips/) cover both.

It is also worth knowing that opinion runs both ways. In the balancing thread, one player says they walk through everything at level 68 and finds the game too easy later on. If the first region is your wall, the evidence from those threads is that it is a wall many players climbed. Fix the route, wear a Shell, give yourself a way to heal, and turn on the safety tools the patches added.
