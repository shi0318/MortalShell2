The October 30 Rotten Autumn hotfix changes the assumptions behind several Mortal Shell II builds. Lazlo's protection no longer persists through shell switches or accumulates through repeated use, an invincibility exploit involving Proxima has been fixed, and the Waning Stone receives a resolve-cost improvement. If a recent build suddenly feels weaker, first determine whether it depended on behavior that the update intentionally removed. This guide proposes a practical rebuild process: preserve the useful parts of your loadout, test its resource cycle, and replace unreliable habits before attempting another demanding dungeon.

## Make a record of the build you actually use
Open the equipment screens and write down your shell, melee weapon, and equipped Tarstones. Describe the purpose of each choice in a few words. A protection effect, a resolve refund, and a damage tool occupy different jobs even if all three appeared in a popular setup. If you cannot say what an item contributes, leave it unchanged for the first comparison. You need a baseline before deciding which component failed. A remembered video title is less useful than the exact equipment on the character you are controlling.

Choose a familiar enemy or short route for testing. Avoid beginning with a new Rotten Autumn dungeon, where unfamiliar layouts and encounters introduce too many variables. Spend one encounter observing incoming damage, your ability cost, and the recovery between actions. The aim is not a perfect damage demonstration. It is to discover whether the loadout completes a realistic combat loop without assuming an effect remains active after it should end. Record what happens before replacing anything, especially if your old setup involved frequent shell switching.

## Remove Lazlo's persistent-protection assumption
The hotfix prevents Lazlo's armor protection from remaining after switching shells or stacking through repeated use. Build around the protection available during its intended state, rather than treating it as a permanent account-wide buff. If your routine started by activating protection and immediately moving to a different shell, change that opening first. Observe the protection indicator and the current shell. Repeating the old activation sequence more times should not be your troubleshooting method for behavior the update explicitly removes.

Relearn the first dangerous exchange without the inherited protection. Approach an enemy with enough room to disengage, defend its opening, and use a short punish. If the new damage intake is uncomfortable, shorten your aggression before changing the entire build. This lets you distinguish dependence on a removed interaction from a generally unsuitable weapon. Once the ordinary encounter feels manageable, reintroduce the shell changes your route needs and check their resource cost. The strongest replacement is a reliable sequence you can perform, rather than another unverified promise of permanent safety.

## Retest Proxima at the traversal transition
Two related fixes matter for Proxima users: a weapon could become unequipped around her ability, including when entering a traversal gate immediately afterward, and an invincibility exploit has been removed. Before a long expedition, use the ability in a controlled location, inspect your equipped melee weapon, and cross a familiar gate normally. Confirm the weapon remains available afterward. This turns a frightening mid-run surprise into a brief equipment check and avoids mistaking an old unequip bug for a new combat restriction.

Do not plan a dangerous route around invulnerability that no longer exists. Practice the ability's intended role and leave room for a defensive response when it ends. If your weapon still disappears on the updated build, record the exact sequence and whether the gate was involved. Equip it through the normal interface when possible and avoid destroying the save or changing unrelated files. A persistent reproducible equipment problem deserves a precise report; an exploit being removed calls for a different combat plan. The two situations should not be diagnosed as the same failure.

## Compare resolve spending before choosing a winner
The Waning Stone's resolve cost is reduced by ten percent per level in the hotfix notes. That is a specific balance change, not permission to multiply discounts into an invented maximum reduction. Read the stone's current level and displayed effect, then test the action it modifies. Check how much resolve is available before and after the action. If the interface rounds values, compare several repeatable uses rather than drawing a confident conclusion from a single ambiguous display change.

The Channeler's Stone also receives a fix involving missing resolve refunds with certain weapons. Retest the weapon you actually use before assuming the refund remains broken. Keep your shell and the rest of your equipment constant while comparing resource behavior. Ask whether the build can fund the action at the moment you need it, not merely whether its theoretical cost is attractive. A discount that leaves you unable to respond to an enemy is less useful than a modest refund supporting a stable attack-and-defense rhythm.

## Treat summons and shades as observable effects
The update fixes invisible shell summons on first use and ensures Rotten Autumn Shell Shades despawn when you are knocked out of your shell. Summon visibility therefore matters for reading the battlefield, while shade persistence should not be assumed through a knockout. In a test encounter, watch where the summon appears and whether the effect remains after the relevant state ends. Avoid interpreting an absent visual as proof of invisible bonus damage. Use the information the updated game actually displays.

If an effect disappears after a knockout, rebuild your immediate recovery around your remaining tools. Give yourself space, re-establish the equipment state, and identify the next safe opening. Recasting everything at once can waste resources and hide which effect failed to return. A simple order is easier to verify: regain control, confirm the current shell and weapon, establish defense, then spend on offense. This is especially valuable in a new dungeon where several enemies can pressure the recovery and make menu-heavy improvisation dangerous.

## Choose a stable expedition loadout
After the test, compare the loadout on three practical questions. Can it survive the opening without an exploit? Can it fund the action you depend on? Can you recover after leaving the expected state, such as losing a shade or changing shells? If one answer is no, address that weakness directly. Do not replace every item because a familiar combo lost its unintended protection. A smaller change makes it easier to identify why the revised setup feels better and whether the benefit holds outside the test encounter.

Finish with the familiar short route before entering new content. Use normal movement, take the gate you usually take, and include the ability sequence you expect to rely on. Keep notes about the interaction rather than chasing a single peak damage number. The hotfix rewards builds whose defense and resource flow make sense in ordinary play. Once your equipment behaves predictably, Rotten Autumn's additional encounters become a chance to explore a working build instead of an expensive experiment in whether last week's exploit still survives.
